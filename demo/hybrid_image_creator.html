Hybrid Image Creator

The Marilyn–Einstein illusion (Oliva, Torralba & Schyns, 2006): blend the low-frequency content of one image with the high-frequency content of another. Up close, the high-frequency image dominates what you perceive; from a distance or squinting, only the low-frequency image survives. Upload your own two images below — nothing leaves your browser.

Step back from the screen (or unfocus your eyes) while looking at the hybrid on the left — it should shift toward Image A. The slider does the same thing on-screen: it physically shrinks the image, the way an object further away actually occupies less of your field of view. Drag it up for more "distance" — exactly the "reduce the size of the image" trick from the lecture, made concrete and adjustable.

Everything is computed locally via a 2D FFT — low-pass keeps only frequencies close to DC, high-pass discards them. Both use Gaussian cutoffs specifically (not ideal/Butterworth) because a hybrid image needs to look natural rather than showing filter ringing.
